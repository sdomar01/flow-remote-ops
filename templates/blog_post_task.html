If one task disappeared tomorrow, which would it be?

Ask most founders this question and the answer is rarely the one they expect. Here is how to find yours, and what to do with it.

Every business owner has a task they quietly wish would go away. It is rarely the big strategic work. It is the repeating, necessary, low-reward work that fills the edges of the day: the inbox, the receipts, the posting schedule, the follow-ups. Because it is small, it is easy to ignore, and because it repeats, it adds up.

The five usual suspects

1. Inbox and emails

Replying, sorting, and following up can quietly take over the day. If you open your inbox "just for a minute" and lose an hour, this may be your task. A remote specialist can sort, draft replies, flag what needs you, and keep the rest moving. Learn more about executive and admin support.

2. Bookkeeping

Receipts, reconciliations, and month-end catch-up are easy to postpone and stressful to face later. If your books are always a few weeks behind, start here. Regular, ongoing support keeps your numbers current so month-end stops being a scramble. See how finance and bookkeeping support works.

3. Content

A content plan that never turns into published content is a common bottleneck. Writing, editing, designing, and scheduling are repeatable work that can be handed off once your brand voice and goals are clear. Explore creative and content support.

4. Scheduling and follow-ups

Back-and-forth on meeting times and reminders adds up. It is small work, done many times, and it interrupts the work that needs your focus. Calendar management and consistent follow-up are among the easiest things to delegate.

5. Something else

Reporting, data entry, CRM updates, research. If you keep doing it yourself because "it's faster than explaining it," it counts. That feeling is usually a sign the task has never been written down.

How to find your one task

  1. Track one week. Write down every task that is not selling, serving clients, or making decisions.
  2. Mark what repeats. Tasks that happen daily or weekly are the best candidates.
  3. Pick the one you dread. The task you keep delaying is usually the one costing you the most.

What to do once you know

Write the task down as a simple process: what triggers it, the steps, and what "done" looks like. A documented task can be handed to a remote specialist without it living in your head. Review the output weekly at first, then less often as it stabilizes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know which task to delegate first?

Start with a task that repeats daily or weekly, follows clear steps, and does not need your personal judgment. Tasks you keep postponing are usually good first candidates.

Do I need to document a task before handing it off?

A short written process helps a lot. Note what triggers the task, the steps involved, and what a finished result looks like. It does not need to be perfect to get started.

Can I hand off just one task instead of building a full team?

Yes. Many businesses start with a single role or a single recurring task and expand support as their needs change.

What if the task I want to remove is not on the usual list?

That is common. Reporting, data entry, CRM updates, and research are frequent examples. If a task repeats and follows steps, it can usually be documented and handed off.
